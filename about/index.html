The story behind Tommy Stories

Tommy Stories started at bedtime.

My dad's name is Thomas. He goes by Tom today, but growing up, he was Tommy. When we were kids, from about age five on, bedtime meant a Tommy Story, and he told us hundreds of them. They wandered through every part of life, and somehow they always found their way to a car.

Growing up in Des Moines

My dad grew up in Des Moines, Iowa. In the '60s and '70s, from about 14 to 20, he worked for his dad, a house painter, and some of the best Tommy Stories came from those painting jobs.

They drove to jobs in a brown van with a three-speed on the floor. He rode in the back, perched on a bucket, with no seatbelt in sight. Iowa winters were hard on that van. It needed sandbags in the back to grip the road, and I still remember the story about the day it hit ice and slid right down the street.

Then there was the family trip out west to Mount Rushmore, in the over-cab camper on a 1974 Ford F-100. He was riding up above the cab when he got sick, and the only way to tell his parents was through the sliding window between the camper and the cab. If you've ever tried to explain that through a little window, you know it isn't easy.

The lime green Camaro

At 16, he bought a 1976 Camaro Type LT with a 350 V8, painted lime green. Later he sold it to buy a Bronco, which was stolen at Iowa State.

We never forgot that Camaro. It took more than 15 years of looking, but we found one just like it: a 1976 Type LT with the 350 V8, in that same lime green, a color you almost never see. It was in decent shape and free of rust, perfect for fixing up.

We have the moment he got it on video, and it's an emotional one. Now he's restoring it himself, a project that will take the next few years.

Drew's Camaro

Some of the stories are our own. My brother Drew's first car, back in high school, was a silver 2000 Camaro SS. Then hard times came, and we had to let it go.

About three years later, I stumbled on a Camaro SS for sale on eBay Motors. Drew had kept the old paperwork, and his Hurst shifter knob was still on it. It was his car. By then our circumstances had changed, and we were able to make an offer and bring it home.

Drew still has it, and he says he'll never sell it again. He just took it on a cross-country road trip.

Why Tommy Stories

It's never really about the car. It's the memories. It's the bond between a father and son, and between brothers. It's so many more things, and that's what Tommy Stories is all about.

Cars carry us through the biggest moments of life: to work and home again, to weddings and hospitals, to church on Sunday and back to the people we love. Every story in Tommy Stories has a car in it, but each one is really about life, family and faith.

We started Tommy Stories so we could all share stories like these openly, and so that reading them might inspire others to grow closer to their faith. Every month, we mail four true stories from readers, in large print, each with a car somewhere in it.
