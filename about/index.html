The story behind Tommy Stories

Tommy Stories started at bedtime.

When my brother and I were little, we looked forward to hearing a Tommy Story before going to sleep. Tommy was our dad. His name is Thomas, and these days most people call him Tom, but growing up, everybody knew him as Tommy.

From the time we were about five years old, Dad told us hundreds of stories about his childhood, his family, and the trouble he got into. Some were funny, some were hard to believe, and a few we'd heard so many times we could almost tell them ourselves.

And for whatever reason, there was almost always a car involved.

Growing up in Des Moines

Dad grew up in Des Moines, Iowa, and spent much of his teenage years helping his father paint houses.

They drove to jobs in an old brown van with a three-speed on the floor. Dad rode in the back, sitting on an overturned bucket among the paint cans. No seatbelts, no proper seat, just a bucket.

Iowa winters were hard on that van. They kept sandbags in the back for traction, and Dad still remembers the day they hit ice and went sliding down the street.

Then there was the family vacation to Mount Rushmore in a camper mounted on a 1974 Ford F-100. Dad's sister was riding above the cab when she got sick. The only way to tell their parents was through a little sliding window between the camper and the truck.

These were the stories we loved hearing, even after we'd heard them a dozen times.

The lime green Camaro

When Dad was sixteen, he bought a 1976 Camaro Type LT with a 350 V8, painted bright lime green. He eventually sold it to buy a Ford Bronco, which was later stolen while he was attending Iowa State.

But he never forgot that Camaro, and neither did we.

For more than fifteen years, we looked for another one just like it. Finding the right year, model, engine, and that unusual shade of green wasn't easy.

Then we finally found one. A rust-free 1976 Type LT with the 350 V8, in the same lime green.

We bought it for Dad and surprised him. We have the moment on video, and it's still emotional to watch. After all those years, he had his Camaro back, or at least one just like it.

He's restoring it himself now, a project that will likely take a few years.

Drew's Camaro

My brother Drew has a Camaro story of his own.

His first car in high school was a silver 2000 Camaro SS. When our family went through some difficult times, we had to sell it.

About three years later, I came across a silver Camaro SS on eBay Motors. Something looked familiar, so we checked the paperwork Drew had saved. Even his old Hurst shifter knob was still there.

It was his car.

Our circumstances had improved, and we were able to buy it back.

Drew still owns it today and says he'll never sell it again. He recently took it on a cross-country road trip, making new memories in the same car he drove in high school.

Why we started Tommy Stories

Looking back, the things I remember most about Dad's stories aren't really the cars. They're the memories of working with Grandpa, family vacations, growing up, and the people who were part of those years.

That's where the idea for Tommy Stories came from.

We started thinking about how many other families must have stories just like ours. Stories about life, family, faith, and the things we go through together.

Maybe it's your first car, a trip to church with your grandmother, or a long drive home after hearing difficult news. Somewhere there's a car, but the story is really about the people and what happened along the way.

We believe these ordinary experiences can bring us closer to one another and remind us of God's presence in our lives.

Each month, Tommy Stories brings you four true stories from everyday people, printed in large type in a booklet you can hold, enjoy, and pass along.

You don't have to be a writer to have a good story. Some of the best ones we've ever heard came from Dad sitting at the edge of our beds when we were little.

And after all these years, we're still telling Tommy Stories.

— The Tommy Stories Family
