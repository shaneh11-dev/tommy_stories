Doug DeBerti once owned the sort of Cadillac that stops people in their tracks: a 1960 Series 62 convertible finished entirely in black.
Black paint. Black top. Black interior. A long, low body with the unmistakable tailfins of its era.
He loved it. But in the early 2000s, his family needed money for their home, and keeping the Cadillac no longer made sense.
Doug sold the car. The money covered about five months of house payments.
He believed he had made the right decision. His family came first.
That did not mean he forgot the car.
For roughly twenty years, the Cadillac remained one of the automobiles he most wished he could have kept. He sometimes told the story to his son, Brad, who shared his father's enthusiasm for building and working on vehicles.
Eventually, Brad urged him to find out what had happened to it.
Doug contacted the man who had purchased the Cadillac all those years before. To his amazement, the owner was thinking about selling.
Even better, the price was not much higher than what Doug had received two decades earlier.
Father and son traveled to California to collect the car. Doug did not really know what condition he would find.
The years could have been cruel. Cars rust. Interiors decay. Engines seize. A twenty-year reunion with a beloved old automobile can end in disappointment.
Doug approached the Cadillac and began examining it.
~~~
The car was almost exactly as he remembered.
It had been kept in a garage, protected from weather. The body had no rust. The original paperwork was still with the car. Even the convertible top worked.
He looked at the odometer. When he had sold it, the Cadillac showed about 60,000 miles. Its careful second owner had added only about 8,000 miles in twenty years.
Doug could scarcely contain his excitement.
He had sacrificed the car to protect his family, and now that same family had helped him bring it home.
There were small jobs to do: a few body imperfections, some interior wear, and safety improvements he hoped to make. But the Cadillac was still a running, driving car.
For the trip home, Doug settled behind the wheel of the big convertible. Brad followed in a Chevrolet Silverado.
Imagine what those miles must have represented: twenty years of wondering, a hard financial choice, and the relief of discovering that something precious had been cared for.
Doug said he did not intend to sell it again.
Sometimes a car comes back because a buyer kept it safe. Sometimes it comes back because a son listens to an old story and decides it is worth pursuing.
This Cadillac returned because both things happened.
