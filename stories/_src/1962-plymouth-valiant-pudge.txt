Glenn Bunting grew up in suburban Detroit with a family car that didn't look much like the other cars on the road.
It was a white 1960 Plymouth Valiant.
On summer evenings, Glenn and the other children piled into the back seat while their father, a Chrysler engineer, drove them to Mac's Party Store.
The routine was simple. Their father bought something for himself, and each child got a pretzel stick.
But one evening, the family outing turned into an adventure.
The Valiant's push-button transmission got stuck in reverse.
Their father couldn't select a forward gear.
Instead of calling for help, he turned around in the driver's seat, looked through the rear window, and drove the family all the way home backward.
When he backed into their driveway, the children cheered.
Glenn never forgot that ride.
He decided that someday, he would own a Valiant of his own.
In 1978, after landing a newspaper job in California, he began searching for one. Seven years passed before he found the right one.
It was a 1962 Valiant, originally owned by a college student's grandparents. The brakes, exhaust, and upholstery needed work, but the car still had its original engine.
The asking price was $800. Glenn negotiated it down to $675.
He was delighted.
His wife Willow was less impressed.
She had once driven a stylish Thunderbird, and this bulbous little Plymouth was not her idea of a dream car.
She nicknamed it Pudge.
The name stayed.
~~~
Over the next twenty-five years, Pudge became part of the family whether Willow had intended it or not.
The car followed them through moves from San Diego to Los Angeles, Maryland, Santa Monica, and back to the Bay Area.
Glenn bought two worn-out Valiants for spare parts, repaired rust, and kept the original Slant Six engine running.
The family's younger son drove Pudge while attending Long Beach State. Their older son later entered it in a San Jose car show.
By 2010, the car was still drawing smiles from strangers. Some remembered the unusual first-generation Valiant. Younger people had never seen anything quite like it.
At one gas station, a teenager looked it over and asked, “What is that thing?”
Questions like that gave Glenn a chance to tell the story of the night his father drove the family home in reverse.
That's what made Pudge special.
The car Glenn bought wasn't the very same Valiant from his childhood. But every drive gave him another chance to tell his father's story.
A funny little mechanical failure had become a family memory.
And decades later, that memory was still going places.
