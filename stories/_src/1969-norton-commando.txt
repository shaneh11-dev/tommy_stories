One Christmas, two brothers gave their father a motorcycle he already owned.
He had not seen it running for nearly twenty years.
The motorcycle was a 1969 Norton Commando, an old British machine that had once been a proud part of their father's life. He had acquired it as payment for a debt, ridden it for years, and eventually parked it when mechanical trouble brought the fun to a halt.
That was in the early 1990s.
Like so many projects, the Norton was taken apart with good intentions. There would be time to repair it later. But family responsibilities and ordinary life intervened.
The motorcycle slowly became a collection of components scattered through the basement. By then, the idea of hearing it run again seemed increasingly remote.
His sons, Jeff and Jason Ledellaytner, knew how much it meant to him.
They also knew their father would probably never expect what they were planning.
They decided to have the Norton restored in secret and give it back for Christmas.
There was a rather obvious obstacle: the motorcycle, or what remained of it, was in their father's own basement.
How could they remove piles of parts without him noticing?
The brothers worked out a plan, carried everything out, and entrusted the dismantled motorcycle to Classic Bike Experience in Essex, Vermont.
There the pieces were examined, repaired, assembled, and brought back to life.
The project took months. Through it all, their father remained unaware that his dormant Norton had disappeared from downstairs.
The brothers filmed the whole effort for a short film called The Norton Project.
Finally the rebuilt motorcycle was ready. Beautifully finished, it looked nothing like the unfinished collection of parts that had occupied the basement.
Now came the part nobody could rehearse.
~~~
At Christmas, the brothers presented their father with the restored Commando.
His reaction, preserved on film, is the sort of moment people remember long after they have forgotten the mechanical details. His sons had quietly taken something he loved, completed the work he had never managed to finish, and returned it to him.
The motorcycle was not a replacement, or merely another example of the same model. It was his Norton.
The family also had a playful detail prepared: a re-created vintage Norton poster featuring their mother in a photograph from her youth.
The surprise was not simply that the bike looked beautiful. The real gift was being able to hear it run and ride it again.
At the end of the film, their father takes the restored Commando out on the road.
Jeff and Jason had given him back a piece of his earlier life. They had also created something new: a family memory recorded for everyone to share.
The best Christmas presents are not always things we have never owned before.
Sometimes they are the things we thought we had lost.
