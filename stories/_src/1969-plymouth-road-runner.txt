While serving in Vietnam, Ronnie Clark had one very specific dream about coming home.
A black Dodge Charger with a white interior and a Hemi engine.
He sent money home to his father so the car would be waiting when his military service ended.
But his father had other ideas.
Instead of the Charger, he bought Ronnie a 1967 Dodge Coronet R/T.
It was a serious muscle car with a big 440 engine. Ronnie still wasn't happy. After a year in a war zone, he had hoped to make at least one decision for himself.
Then, in 1968, he spotted a Plymouth Satellite in a bronze metallic color that caught his eye.
Ronnie went to his local dealership in Alamosa, Colorado, and ordered a 1969 Road Runner in the same shade. This time he insisted on the engine he wanted.
A 426 Hemi.
He added a four-speed transmission, performance gearing, and the more comfortable interior from a GTX.
The car cost about $4,200, and he waited six months for it to arrive.
Ronnie planned to race it.
But life had another plan.
He met the woman who would become his wife.
They married, and the powerful Plymouth became the couple's everyday car. His wife drove it to work. Ronnie washed it nearly every day and waxed it weekly.
Once, he even collected a washing machine in the Road Runner, then drove home over Colorado's Wolf Creek Pass during a blizzard, with tire chains fitted and the appliance in the trunk.
It was hardly the glamorous racing career the Hemi had been ordered for.
Then Ronnie nearly sold it.
~~~
A 1971 Plymouth 'Cuda convertible had caught his eye. He visited a dealer to discuss trading the Road Runner, but was offered just $800.
The deal was too disappointing. He kept the old Plymouth.
Later, a failed water pump took the car off the road, and it spent many years in storage.
Ronnie's son Chris eventually helped get it running again. Instead of becoming a forgotten machine in a garage, the Road Runner returned to car shows and family outings.
By the time a magazine told its story in 2013, Ronnie was recovering from heart surgery. The family still treasured the Hemi.
It's easy to imagine the car as a fearsome drag racer. That was Ronnie's original plan, after all.
But the Road Runner's real achievement was less obvious.
It had carried a young veteran into marriage, hauled a washing machine through a snowstorm, survived a nearly disastrous trade-in, and given father and son something to work on together.
The Hemi had never made its name at the drag strip.
It had spent its life taking care of a family.
