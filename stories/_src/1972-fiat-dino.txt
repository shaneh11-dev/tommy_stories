When Bill first saw a Fiat Dino Coupe in 1967, he decided he had to own one.
There was only one problem. He lived in Yorkshire, England, and at the time the car was sold only to Italians.
Most people would have chosen something easier to buy.
Bill learned Italian.
He began corresponding with a contact in Florence and discovered that buying a new Dino would require membership in an Italian motoring organization. With help from the British Embassy, he joined the Automobile Club Firenze.
Five years after falling for the car, he finally traveled to Italy in 1972 to collect his silver Dino 2400 Coupe.
He withdrew thousands of pounds' worth of Italian lire and hurried to the Fiat office in Florence. Then he drove his new car through Tuscany, getting to know its powerful V6 engine on the roads he had dreamed about.
Bill later called that first drive, alone on a beautiful day with the whole of Tuscany ahead of him, a wonderful experience.
His wife had come along, and at their hotel the proprietor spotted the car from the reception desk and declared it the best car in the world. Then the couple drove the beautiful coupe home to Yorkshire.
But this was never merely a showpiece.
The Dino became part of the family's life. Bill used it for business and family travel, and he saved a remarkable archive of cuttings, brochures, letters, and manuals.
Years passed. The car aged, and so did its owner.
Bill became unwell. There was a place he particularly wanted to see again while he still could.
Florence.
And he wanted to make the journey in the Dino.
This time, though, Bill wouldn't be driving.
~~~
He told his son David that he hoped to return to Italy as a passenger.
In 2007, father and son set off together in the same silver coupe Bill had collected thirty-five years earlier.
David took the wheel. Bill rode beside him.
They reached Florence and drove through the Tuscan hills, retracing the country where Bill's love affair with the car had truly begun.
The original journey had been about a young man's determination to get the car of his dreams.
The second was about a father and son sharing something much harder to replace: time together.
Bill died years later. The Dino remained with David and eventually his family, until it was offered for sale in 2022 after fifty years of family ownership.
Along with the car came its original Florence registration plates and the papers Bill had carefully saved.
They were evidence of an unusual purchase, but also reminders of a remarkable return trip.
Bill had crossed Europe to bring his dream home.
Decades later, his son had driven him back to where it all started.
