One day in the mid-1980s, Glen Horn was walking near his surf shop in San Diego when a strange vehicle rumbled past.
It was a huge old step van, the kind of square-bodied delivery truck most people would barely notice.
Glen couldn't stop looking.
He had spent years traveling the remote beaches of Baja California in search of waves. To him, that odd vehicle looked like the perfect home on wheels.
Two months later, Glen heard that the van's owner wanted to sell. A little later, he learned it wouldn't start.
Glen tracked down the owner and offered $1,000.
The offer was accepted.
He towed the van home, got it running, and began turning it into something no ordinary delivery driver could have imagined.
The vehicle became known as Big Red: an old milk van body on a four-wheel-drive chassis, turned into a home on wheels with room for surfboards and supplies.
Instead of traveling to Baja for a short vacation, Glen wanted to live there for long stretches.
And he wasn't going alone.
His wife, Roberta, shared his affection for the place and the independent life it demanded.
Their days revolved around the ocean, the weather, and keeping a large aging truck functioning far from modern conveniences.
Life in the desert was hardly easy. Supplies needed planning, mechanical problems required patience, and the nearest repair shop could be a long way off.
But Glen had made a choice about what mattered to him.
He wanted time outdoors more than a collection of possessions.
As the years went by, Big Red became their home on the Baja coast for about nine months of every year. With its unmistakable shape, it was part vehicle, part house, and part declaration of independence.
Then a filmmaker came along.
~~~
Eric Ebner's short documentary, The Bull, told the story of Glen and the unusual life he had built around Baja and the ocean.
By early 2026, Glen was in his late sixties and still surfing. His routine was demanding, but he had never stopped seeking the waves that first drew him south.
The film showed that the old van represented more than a clever camping conversion.
It had made a very different kind of life possible.
Glen and Roberta had chosen fewer comforts, more responsibility, and an extraordinary amount of time together in a place they loved.
Some people build their lives around a house and use a vehicle to get away from it.
Glen did almost the reverse.
He found a vehicle, built a life around it, and let the open coastline become his backyard.
For all its size, the old step van carried a remarkably simple idea.
A good life doesn't have to look like everybody else's.
