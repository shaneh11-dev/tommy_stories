For more than forty years, Tom Calhoun kept a 1960 Plymouth Fury he could barely drive.
It was not because he disliked the car. Quite the opposite. The Plymouth had been there for some of the most important moments of his life.
Tom bought it in 1968, after moving across the country. He drove it on dates with the woman who would become his wife. When they married, that same Fury was their wedding car.
Then came an unlucky encounter with a deer. The collision damaged the front of the car. Worse problems were developing beneath the car. The engine was worn, and the transmission was barely able to keep going.
By 1970, the Plymouth was parked. Tom always intended to get it running properly again. But the years filled with work and family responsibilities, and the old car remained out of service.
Decades passed. To anyone else, it might have looked like another abandoned project. To Tom, it was the automobile that had carried him into married life.
In 2011, he handed the Fury over to his son. He did not know what would become of it.
His son, however, had a plan. Rather than let the old Plymouth fade away, he arranged to bring it back to the condition his father remembered.
The effort stayed a secret from Tom.
Then life delivered another blow. Within a few weeks, Tom lost two of his brothers. He was grieving and not much interested in going out, even when a friend persuaded him to meet for coffee.
Soon afterward, Tom's son called with what sounded like an ordinary favor. A television crew was supposedly at a real-estate office in town. They wanted his opinion for a news story about real estate.
Tom went to the office expecting an interview.
~~~
Instead, he saw bright camera lights and something that brought his past rushing back: his beautifully restored 1960 Plymouth Fury.
His son had brought the wedding car home.
The surprise left Tom overwhelmed. After more than four decades, a machine tied to his youth, his marriage, and his family was once again ready for the road.
Not everything had returned to original specifications. The car now had power steering, and its V8 setup differed from the one Tom remembered. He even had a few finishing details he still wanted to correct.
None of that spoiled the experience.
Tom began driving the Plymouth whenever he could. A friend spotted him going through town and later remarked on the enormous smile on his face.
The son had not merely restored an old car. He had returned a piece of his parents' beginning, at a time when his father badly needed something joyful.
The Fury had taken Tom to his wedding decades earlier. Now it was giving him another reason to look forward to the road ahead.
